Ethics section: "Throughout the study, the research team diligently evaluated ethical considerations to ensure that the research was carried out responsibly and in accordance with established ethical standards. Efforts were made to reduce the potential risks to participants, safeguard data privacy, and maintain transparency in our processes and results.\r

  When implementing this research result practically, it is essential to assess its effects on videoforensic subjects. No table areas impacted include: (1) The distribution of forensic videos might lead to the public perceiving the subjects’ im ages and behaviors within a defined range, potentially causing unpredictable personal repercussions. (2) Employing a LED device alongside natural or ambient lighting during forensics could produce visual effects on filmed subjects.\r

  With regard to point (1), we suggest improvements in two areas: First, advance notice should be provided before video forensics occur. In the case of continuous and unbroken video monitoring in public spaces (such as CCTV), clear signs or informational warnings should be displayed when individuals enter the surveillance area. For specific forensic recordings (such as notarization of a will), more detailed notifications should be offered through signed legal documents, etc. Secondly, standalone technical tools should be created to allow third parties to detect whether the current environment is subject to forensic application of this technology; for example, an app using high-speed shutter photography could be developed to identify high-frequency light modulation for covert channel communication in the environment.\r

  Regarding point (2), the LED light modulation signal strength in this technical solution is of moderate level (based on the conclusion that an SNR above-18.19dB is adequate), and the high-frequency light modulation uses flicker frequencies undetectable by the average human eye, theoretically posing no harm to the human eye. However, more research is needed to validate this preliminary conclusion, which is a common concern in VLC scenarios."
