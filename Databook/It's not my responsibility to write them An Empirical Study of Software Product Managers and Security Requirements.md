Ethics section: "Participants were recruited via the Upwork platform. All participants were already registered on the platform before and independently of our study and agreed to its privacy policy.\r

  Following recruitment, the participants were forwarded to our organization’s survey server. The data provided in the study was collected on this survey system approved by our organization. The study data was not visible to the recruitment platform. Before any data was collected as part of our survey, participants were presented with a GDPR-compliant consent form that had been agreed with our data protection officer. Among other things, this contained information about the study’s objectives, data handling practices, duration, and potential risks. Participants were informed of their right to withdraw at any time without consequences and assured that only pseudonymized data and quotations would be used in any publications. After completing tasks and surveys, participants were debriefed on the study’s aim to investigate how product management roles address SRs in documentation creation.\r

  As part of the study, care was taken to ensure that even the pseudonymized data could not be traced back to individuals or organizations. Accordingly, fictitious scenarios were deliberately used to ensure that participants did not disclose any sensitive information.\r

  To finally ensure that our study meets the ethical scientific requirements in its entirety, we have submitted it to our institutional ethics board. This study was approved under the reference number 2024-1255 by the ethical committee Bad Oeynhausen on 30.08.2024. The ethical review also included the data protection declaration, the methodological approach, and the data analysis. As part of the ethical review, the fol lowing risks for the participants (a-c) were weighed against the following opportunities (i-iv): (a) Questionnaire risks: Boredom, fatigue, mild stress, (b) Data breaches: Despite IT security expertise, there is a residual risk of data breaches, (c) Reputational damage: If participants drop out of the study on their own, there could be a capture by Upwork, (i) Financial: $45 compensation for a 60-minute survey, (ii) Reputation: study participants can add another successful project to their Upwork profile, (iii) Learning effect: building knowledge in the IT security field by debriefing with regard to the scenarios and (iv) Research: gaining insight into how product owners deal with IT SRs."
